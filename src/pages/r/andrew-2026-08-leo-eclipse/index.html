Vocabulary
Sign  ·  element  ·  modality
The twelve zodiac signs (Aries, Taurus, … Leo, … Aquarius, etc.) are sorted two ways:
  • Element — what the sign feels like. Fire (spirit, will, courage) · Earth (body, practicality, form) · Air (mind, ideas, exchange) · Water (emotion, sensitivity, memory).
  • Modality — what the sign does. Cardinal (starts things) · Fixed (holds things steady) · Mutable (adapts and changes).
Leo, the sign of this eclipse — and the sign of your Ascendant and Sun — is fixed fire. It holds its ground and it burns with heart. This is the sign the eclipse is happening in, and it is your sign in both of the most important slots of the chart.
Vocabulary
Natal  ·  Ascendant
Natal means at the moment of your birth. Your natal chart is a snapshot of the whole sky at that moment. Your Ascendant (also called the "rising sign") is the sign that was coming up over the eastern horizon at your first breath — it is the most personal, body-level part of the chart. For you, that rising sign is Leo, and your Sun is also in Leo. When this reading says the eclipse "lands on your Ascendant" or "in your 1st house," it means the eclipse is happening in the most personal, body-and-self region of your chart.

Eclipses pulse. They are not steady weather. They accelerate what has been slow, expose what has been hidden, and force events to happen faster than they otherwise would. Around eclipse windows, doors that were closed swing open (or the reverse), decisions that have been marinating for months present themselves as forks in the road, and people show up in your life or fall away unexpectedly. Nothing predictable — that's the wrong word — but quickened.

You will not see this eclipse from Springfield — the path of totality runs across the Atlantic, Iceland, and northern Spain — and the sky over your head may only dim slightly, if at all. That does not weaken the astrology. Eclipses activate charts whether or not the person can see them. It only means the work is happening in your chart and in your circumstances, not in the sky over the field.

Vocabulary
Benefic  (and its opposite, malefic)
Traditional astrology sorts the planets into two rough temperaments. The benefics — Jupiter and Venus — are the helpful planets: they bring ease, opportunity, growth, connection. The malefics — Mars and Saturn — are the difficult planets: they bring friction, discipline, limits, effort. Neither is good or evil. Benefics can spoil you; malefics can make you strong. Jupiter is called the great benefic because it is the sky's most generous planet. You are a day chart, which means Jupiter is your gentler benefic — the sky's blessing on you shows up as Jupiter contacts.

Meet week at a glance

Vocabulary
Perfect  ·  perfects  ·  perfection
When two things in the sky reach the exact angle they've been moving toward, that alignment is said to perfect. It is astrology's word for ripens. Before it perfects, the effect is building; at the moment it perfects, the effect is at its peak; after, it begins to release. When the reading says a transit “perfects at 8:46 PM,” that is the moment it is most fully cooked.
Vocabulary
Aspect  ·  the five kinds of contact
An aspect is the geometric angle between two points in the sky. Different angles create different flavors of contact.
  • Conjunction (0°) — two things in the same place. Fused. Blended.
  • Sextile (60°) and trine (120°) — supportive, flowing, easy angles.
  • Square (90°) — tension, friction, a challenge that produces growth.
  • Opposition (180°) — face-to-face, on opposite sides of the sky.

What this reading is not

It is not a prediction of your placing. Astrology does not tell you what will happen; it tells you what season you are in, what the sky is asking, what forces are moving. Whether you medal, whether you make a specific cut, whether a specific opponent falls or rises — that is not what this reading can or should tell you. What it can tell you is which day to push, which morning to trust your process, which evening to pace yourself around three stacked transits, and how to read the strange feeling in your body at 1 PM Wednesday as this eclipse resetting my body instead of something is wrong with me.

One last thing. An eclipse in your 1st house, exact-trine your Lord of the Year, with Jupiter sitting on your natal Sun, is a decade-level configuration. Vocabulary in this appendix
A few more astrology words
  • Orb — the tightness of an alignment, in degrees. An orb of 0.33° means two points are lined up almost exactly. An orb of 3° means they are close, but not touching. Tighter orb = louder effect.
  • Ascendant / Descendant / Midheaven — the three most important angles of a chart: the eastern horizon at your birth (Ascendant), the western horizon (Descendant), and the top of the sky (Midheaven). They anchor the whole chart.
  • Retrograde  (marked "R") — when a planet appears, from Earth, to be moving backwards.
  • North Node — a mathematical point that traditionally marks a direction of growth.
  • Lot of Fortune  ·  Lot of Spirit — calculated points that describe, respectively, the body's fortune and the soul's calling.
  • Sect — whether you were born during the day or at night. Sect changes which planets are gentler for you and which are harsher. You are a day chart, which means Jupiter is your gentler benefic and Mars is your harsher malefic.
  • Whole-sign houses  ·  tropical zodiac  ·  Hellenistic — the mathematical and traditional framework used to build the chart. See the sources note at the end.
